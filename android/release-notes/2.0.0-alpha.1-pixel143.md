## What's changed

- **Cover Bluetooth bursts at the 100 Hz output cadence.** Pixel142 held its
  10 ms sensor request and measured about 99 Hz at the parser, with no startup
  tracking timeout in the first check. Packets nevertheless arrived in roughly
  30 ms bursts, leaving output near 70 Hz under the previous 20 ms age limit.
  This preview advances the latest pose to the current host time for at most
  40 ms, using its measured body-axis velocity. It adds no future lookahead or
  audio buffering.
- **Keep source freshness honest.** Calculated poses retain their original
  timestamps and discontinuity. The existing 250 ms stale-source deadline,
  source-identity checks, counter/clock guards, calibration and 100 Hz pacing
  remain unchanged. Output slots older than the reconstruction limit are still
  skipped.

The underlying Bluetooth callback stream remains bursty. This is bounded pose
reconstruction, not a claim of evenly delivered physical samples. It assumes
velocity stays constant between reports; longer missing bursts and abrupt stops
can still produce tracking error.

AAC-ELD/PCM32, audio buffers, volume, calls, saved calibration, native preferences
and the separately approved Pixel-wide prediction profile are unchanged. Dolby
head-tracking integration remains separate. Native head tracking is a DEBUG
preview.

## Validation and remaining checks

A bounded timestamp-only trace confirmed all 1,837 app pose writes reached root,
with about 0.4 ms mean pipe delivery. Most long motion gaps occurred inside socket
receive calls. No root pacing cap explained the missing updates.

Replay of those recorded write times estimated about 74 Hz coverage at 20 ms and
99 Hz at 40 ms. These are optimistic software estimates, not live measurements.
Actual compiled-helper and pinned native-predictor tests checked original
timestamps, quaternion math, stale deadlines, bursts and head stops. A synthetic
abrupt stop during an 88 ms callback blackout showed greater overshoot at 40 ms
than at 30 ms; the longer limit is being offered for a controlled comparison.

Tests cover the inclusive 40 ms boundary, rejection immediately beyond it,
100 Hz burst coverage and long-gap skips without renewing freshness. Sustained
device delivery and movement quality must still pass before calling this stable.

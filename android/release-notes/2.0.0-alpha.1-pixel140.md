## What's changed

- **Keep tracking through valid turns.** A controlled Pixel capture showed tracking
  stopping at the converter's raw-quaternion cutoff. A separate half-turn capture
  then confirmed that Pro 3 correctly flips all three transmitted components when
  crossing that boundary. The converter now accepts those equivalent orientations
  while retaining clock, counter, packet-range, reference-jump and freshness checks.
  A valid identity orientation after startup is accepted too.
- **Start from a stable orientation reference.** Each tracking session anchors its
  output at the first valid head pose. Ordinary head movement stays near the center
  of Android's orientation representation. Body-axis angular velocity and saved
  mounting calibration are preserved. Calibration averages also align equivalent
  quaternion signs before combining samples.
- **Reduce repeated-pose steps.** Android's periodic reports can otherwise reuse an
  older pose between Bluetooth bursts. The provider now advances that pose to the
  current host time using its measured body-axis velocity, for at most 20 ms. It
  adds no future lookahead to Android's own prediction. Older slots are skipped;
  calculated poses never renew the original source's freshness deadline.
- **Make diagnostics precise.** Cached counters distinguish source reuse, actual
  held poses, calculated updates and skipped slots. A latched converter failure
  retains its exact reason for inspection instead of only reporting Motion Changed.

This remains a **DEBUG head-tracking preview**. Existing calibrations, native
preferences, AAC-ELD/PCM32, codec buffers, volume and call routing are preserved.
The HID descriptor and Android's requested report cadence are unchanged. Dolby
decoder and hollow-sound work is tracked separately.

## Validation

The real boundary capture contains 457 nonzero motion reports, including four
canonical quaternion sign flips and 123 reports inside the old rejected range.
Replay of that exact capture produced 456 valid poses; the previous converter
stopped after 100. All emitted poses passed the codec and freshness checks.
Tests cover equivalent quaternion signs, session references, calibration across
the boundary, angular rates, bounded integration, stale data, skipped-slot pacing,
source ownership and diagnostic counters. CI also runs Android lint, native audio
checks, and full signed AAC-ELD/PCM32 package verification.

The 5 ms request targets 200 Hz. Previous live timing measured about 160 fresh
reports per second reaching the app, with burst delivery; this is not proof of
steady 200 Hz delivery or acoustic latency. Audible smoothness and live recovery
need a comparison with this build. Session centering does not change Android's
native predictor or establish its behavior during a complete body rotation.
